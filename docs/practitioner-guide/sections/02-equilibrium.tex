\section{Spatial equilibrium and market access}
\label{ch:equilibrium}

\subsection{Locations, production, and consumption}

There are \(N\) locations. Location \(i\) has labor \(L_i\), wage \(w_i\),
productivity \(A_i\), and amenity \(u_i\). A differentiated variety is produced
with labor under constant returns and perfect competition. Consumers have CES
preferences with elasticity \(\sigma>1\). Indirect utility in location \(i\)
is proportional to
\[
  U_i=\frac{w_i u_i}{P_i},
\]
where \(P_i\) is the CES price index.

The economic-geography specification allows local activity to affect
productivity and amenities:
\[
  A_i=\bar A_i L_i^\alpha,
  \qquad
  u_i=\bar u_i L_i^\beta.
\]
The parameter \(\alpha\) captures a productivity agglomeration force and
\(\beta\) an amenity or dispersion force. Labor is perfectly mobile across
locations, aggregate labor is fixed, and each worker supplies one unit
inelastically. The spatial equilibrium equalizes utility across occupied
locations and clears goods and labor markets.

These assumptions are not innocuous labels. Perfect mobility determines the
welfare closure used in the paper. Fixed aggregate labor and labor-only
production rule out additional factor-income and labor-supply channels. The
software's urban specification uses a different state and welfare row rather
than relabeling these objects.

\subsection{Bilateral trade and network costs}

Let \(\tau_{ij}\) be the composite iceberg cost of delivering a good from
\(i\) to \(j\). Bilateral expenditure has the gravity form
\[
 X_{ij}
 =W^{1-\sigma}
 \left(\frac{A_i u_j}{\tau_{ij}}\right)^{\sigma-1}
 w_i^{1-\sigma}w_j^\sigma L_j,
\]
where \(W\) is aggregate welfare in the normalization used by the paper. The
network determines \(\tau_{ij}\); the spatial equilibrium determines the
levels of wages, labor, productivity, and amenities that enter bilateral
trade.

The model admits a useful recursive representation. Define transformed inward
and outward market-access stocks
\[
  \mathcal P_i=P_i^{1-\sigma},
  \qquad
  \mathcal Q_i=\Pi_i^{1-\sigma}.
\]
Their exact construction is given in the paper's Appendix A. Each object
satisfies a local recursion. Market access at one node equals a local absorption
term plus weighted market access at adjacent nodes. This converts a dense
bilateral object into a sparse network system.

\subsection{Trade exposure and traffic factorization}

Define location \(i\)'s trade exposure by
\[
  \mathcal T_i=\frac{\mathcal P_i\mathcal Q_i}{Y^W},
  \qquad
  Y^W=\sum_i w_iL_i.
\]
The term is a stock of trade opportunities reaching the location before local
absorption. In older computational notes it is called a node-visit or
occupation stock. The guide uses the economics-facing term ``trade exposure.''

Let \(\mu_{ij}\) be the outward continuation share from \(i\) to \(j\), and
let \(\lambda^{in}_{ij}\) be the corresponding inward share at \(j\). Directed
edge traffic factors as
\[
  \Xi_{ij}=\mu_{ij}\mathcal T_i
          =\lambda^{in}_{ij}\mathcal T_j
          =\frac{\kappa_{ij}^{1-\sigma}
                  \mathcal P_i\mathcal Q_j}{Y^W}.
\]
This identity connects the theoretical recursion to observed flows. Outgoing
and incoming traffic totals satisfy
\[
 O_i=\sum_j\Xi_{ij}=(1-s_i^x)\mathcal T_i,
 \qquad
 I_i=\sum_j\Xi_{ji}=(1-s_i^y)\mathcal T_i,
\]
where \(s_i^x\) and \(s_i^y\) are local absorption shares.

In theory, either side recovers \(\mathcal T_i\). In empirical work the two
recoveries can disagree because flows and margins come from different
sources. The generic loader rejects disagreement beyond the declared
tolerance. Any balancing or projection must occur in a documented
preprocessing step.

\subsection{The transformed equilibrium}

The appendix works with two transformed location-level variables,
\[
 x_i=w_i^\sigma L_i^{1+\beta(\sigma-1)},\qquad
 y_i=W^{\sigma-1}w_i^{1-\sigma}L_i^{\alpha(\sigma-1)}.
\]
The transformation isolates the sparse market-access propagation from the
aggregate labor constraint. Define
\[
 e=1+\beta(\sigma-1)+\alpha\sigma,
 \qquad
 \rho=\frac{1+\alpha+\beta}{e}.
\]
The inverse map from \((x,y)\) to wages, labor, and welfare exists only if
\(e\neq0\), and the welfare normalization also requires
\(1+\alpha+\beta\neq0\).

The code enforces these restrictions before constructing the model. It also
checks the condition number of each equilibrium closure. A finite formula on
paper is not enough when the corresponding numerical system is almost
singular.

\implementationbox{The coefficient map is implemented by
\code{AdjointRSUE.coefs}. The no-congestion Jacobian is assembled by
\code{AdjointRSUE.assemble\_J}. The generic economic-geography builder then
adds route, modal, and congestion feedback through the common transport
closure.}
